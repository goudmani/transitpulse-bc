Service volume follows the working day

Arrivals per hour, Vancouver local time. The 46× swing between 3am and the afternoon peak is the feed accurately reflecting how many buses are on the road.

Delay does not follow volume

Mean and 90th-percentile delay by hour. The busiest hours are not the worst ones — the 2pm spike sits well before peak volume, and the quietest hour of the night carries a higher mean delay than the morning rush.

This chart exposed a bug. hour_of_day is derived from observed_arrival_ts, which is UTC. But PEAK_HOURS = {7, 8, 15, 16, 17} in gold_features.py was written for local hours.

In UTC those are 00:00, 01:00, 08:00, 09:00 and 10:00 Vancouver time — so is_peak currently flags midnight and mid-morning, and misses the entire afternoon rush.

Worse, the serving path computes its hour from now + LOCAL_OFFSET (−7h) before calling the same is_peak_hour(). Training reads UTC, serving reads local: the two disagree by seven hours for the same bus. That is textbook training/serving skew, and it is exactly what tests/test_feature_parity.py exists to catch.
